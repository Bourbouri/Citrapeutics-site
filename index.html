Unlocking ACOD1 for Cancer Immunotherapy

First-in-class. First-on-target. Oral small molecules.

Citrapeutics is a preclinical-stage venture developing novel inhibitors of ACOD1 (IRG1), a newly validated immunotherapeutic target. Our orally administered small molecules aim to restore anti-tumor immunity across multiple solid tumor types.

The Science

ACOD1 (Aconitate Decarboxylase 1, also known as IRG1) is an enzyme highly expressed in tumor-associated myeloid cells. It produces itaconic acid, a metabolite that suppresses anti-tumor immune responses and promotes an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.

By inhibiting ACOD1, Citrapeutics' compounds aim to reprogram myeloid cells toward a pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor phenotype—restoring the immune system's ability to recognize and attack cancer cells. Our platform has demonstrated significant preclinical evidence in three independent mouse models as a monotherapy.

A Targeted, Indication-Agnostic Approach: ACOD1 serves as both the therapeutic target and a predictive biomarker. Our target patient population includes individuals with high tumor tissue ACOD1 expression or high levels of circulating itaconic acid in the bloodstream.

Current indications with high tumor tissue ACOD1 expression include:

Breast Cancer Head & Neck Cancer Stomach Cancer Lung Cancer Colon Cancer Skin Cancer
